Rebuild Answers / Method

How every number on this site is built

Two scored things: share of answer, and agent surface. Both are opinions expressed as arithmetic. This page says whose opinion, and why — so you can argue with the method instead of the number.

Also published as scoring.md in the open-source repo · last revised 21 Sep 2026

Share of answer

Appearances are weighted by position, not counted flat

Being named as the recommendation and being listed fourth are not the same business outcome, so they don't score the same.

PositionWeightWhy
first — named as the recommendation1.0The answer. Most readers stop here.
listed — one option among several0.5Real, but you're the alternative.
mention_only — referenced, not offered0.2Visible, not considered.
absent0

SoA = Σ(weight per appearance) ÷ (prompts × runs)

The 0.5 for listed is a judgement call. You could argue 0.4 or 0.6 and we wouldn't fight you. What isn't arguable is that it has to sit below first and above mention_only.

Presence is a fraction, never a tick

These systems are non-deterministic. A brand can appear in run 1 and vanish in run 2 for the same prompt. One run is close to worthless as evidence, so every prompt runs three times and you get the fraction.

StabilityWhat it meansWhat to do
3/3You own this answerProtect it. Watch for framing drift.
2/3Reliable but not lockedFine. Don't spend here.
1/3Pulled in by luckCheapest fix on your list. Small changes will hold it.
0/3InvisibleThe work queue.

Collapsing 1/3 and 3/3 into a green "present" tick throws away the most actionable column in the whole dataset. Most tools do exactly that.

Share of answer is share of your prompt set

Whoever picks the prompts picks the number. This is the single biggest integrity risk in this category and it belongs at the top of the page, not in a footnote.

Add ten easy prompts and the line goes up while nothing real changed. So:

  • The prompt set is frozen for the engagement.
  • If it has to change, it's versioned, both are kept, and both numbers are reported during the transition month.
  • Every report states the set, the version, the date, and the engine queried.

An agency that quietly reshuffles its prompt universe to make the trendline go up is running a scam with extra steps. The client works it out eventually, and then nothing that agency ever measured is believed again.

We say which engine, every time

Our probe currently queries one engine: Claude with server-side web search. That is a real answer engine and a reasonable proxy for the others, and it is not ChatGPT.

What generalizes between engines is the source layer — which domains get retrieved — because they're all drinking from a similar web. What doesn't generalize is exact phrasing, ordering, and who gets named first. So cartel findings travel; position findings don't. No report of ours says "AI search" when it means one engine.

Agent surface

100 points, and why they're split that way

ComponentPointsReasoning
Content without JS30Everything else is theoretical if a raw fetch returns a shell. Most common failure and the most consequential.
Machine-readable facts20The gap between describable and actionable. An agent can't quote a price it can't parse — and neither can a model.
llms.txt quality15Graded on content, not presence. A 400-link dump scores near zero.
Task completability15Can an agent finish signup, booking or checkout, or does step 4 need a human.
Service discovery10Conditional. Dropped for non-API sites, and the rest rescaled.
Policy coherence10Whether your crawler policy matches your stated business goal.
Bonus: markdown negotiation+5Early capability. Never a deduction.

Why 30 points on the JavaScript check?

Because it's the one that's actually true about most sites and nobody wants to hear it. A client will happily discuss llms.txt for an hour and go quiet when you show them their homepage renders 40 words to a plain HTTP client.

It also gets worse as agents matter more. A crawler that gets a shell comes back later. An agent working against a task budget does not.

What happens when a check doesn't apply?

It's dropped and the remainder is rescaled to 100 — and the report says which ones were dropped, inline, in the score line. A local service business with no API shouldn't be marked down for lacking an MCP card, and shouldn't have to guess whether it was.

What's deliberately not scored?

Content Signals keys we don't recognise are notes, never errors — the IETF draft is still moving, and the spec author's own site uses a key that isn't in the published set. Penalising a site for tracking a draft standard faster than the standard does is bad measurement. Markdown negotiation is bonus-only for the same reason.

Limits

What none of this measures

Volume. The engines do not publish how many people ask any given prompt.

Being precise here matters, because the big platforms have a real answer to it. Ahrefs' Brand Radar and Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit both run prompt databases in the 130M–260M range and model demand from observed search behaviour — "search-backed prompts, not synthetic," in Ahrefs' phrasing. That is a legitimate methodology and it is not something we can match at our scale.

What it is not is measured prompt volume. It's demand inferred from adjacent search data, and the inference is load-bearing. So our honest position is narrower than "nobody knows": nobody observes it directly, large platforms model it from search demand at scale, and we don't model it at all.

We build prompt sets from first-party evidence instead — your sales calls, lost-deal notes, support tickets, community threads — and cite the evidence rather than a number. "14 distinct Reddit threads in six months, near-identical phrasing" is a claim you can go and verify. It is deliberately a smaller claim than 1,200/mo, and a checkable one.

Different instrument, not a better one. It's a weaker-looking claim and a stronger real one.
