Rebuild Answers / How we work

Ninety days, in the order that actually works

Month one moves what you already control. Months two and three build the things that take time to land. Anyone promising AI visibility in thirty days is selling you month one and hoping you don't ask about the rest.

  1. 01 Days 1–30 · own what you own

    Measure, then fix the things that need nobody's permission

    We build the prompt set from your language — sales calls, lost-deal notes, support tickets, the questions your team answers on the phone every week. Not keyword tools. Then we run it and see where you actually stand.

    • Prompt universe built and frozen, with the evidence for each prompt
    • Full probe: every prompt, three runs, verbatim answers kept
    • Share of answer against the competitors you named
    • Misinformation report — what the models get wrong about you, traced to the page that taught them
    • Replacement copy for the highest-value pages you're losing

    Most of the fastest wins live here. Stale pages you forgot were indexed, prices nobody can parse, answers that exist only inside a PDF.

  2. 02 Days 31–60 · build the asset

    Publish the thing that earns the citation

    Models cite specifics. Original data, a real number, a dated claim, a straight answer to a question nobody else answered properly. This is where we make something worth retrieving rather than optimising something that isn't.

    • The one genuinely citable asset your category is missing
    • Schema and machine-readable facts shipped alongside it
    • Agent-surface fixes: server-rendered content, parseable prices, a real llms.txt
    • Second probe against the frozen set — early movement shows up here
  3. 03 Days 61–90 · earn the mentions

    Off-site presence, the slow part, done honestly

    Off-site mentions correlate roughly 3× more strongly with AI visibility than backlinks do. They're also the part that takes months, which is exactly why it starts in month one and not month three.

    • Where your buyers actually talk, ranked by whether you can credibly show up
    • What to publish, in what order, and who has to say it
    • Review and directory presence where it genuinely counts
    • Third probe — and the diff, which is the real deliverable

    We say at kickoff that this takes three to six months. Not in month four, when it would sound like an excuse.

What we won't do

The refusals, in public

Off-site mentions being the strongest signal has produced a lot of quiet astroturfing in this category. Ours is written down so you can hold us to it — and so you know what you're not buying.

  • No fake accounts, no sockpuppets, no paid posts dressed as organic. Bans propagate, platforms flag the pattern, and a burned community is permanent.
  • No bought reviews. Asking every customer at a natural moment is legitimate and most companies simply never do it. Selecting only the happy ones is where it turns.
  • No burying a true complaint. If the models say onboarding is confusing because onboarding is confusing, that's product feedback that arrived through a language model. You get it as feedback, not as a campaign.
  • No chasing prompts you can't win. When the honest answer is that a competitor serves that buyer better, we say so and spend the hour somewhere it pays.
  • No reshuffling the prompt set to make the line go up. That's a scam with extra steps, and you'd eventually work it out.

The honest version

Why hire anyone when the tool is free?

Our tooling is open source, MIT, on GitHub. You can run it this afternoon without us, and honestly you should — it'll give you around forty findings.

Thirty-two of them won't matter for your business. Of the eight that do, three are the same problem wearing different hats. We tell you which three, we write the replacement copy, and we measure again in thirty days to see whether it worked.

The audit was never the hard part. Knowing what to ignore is.
