Why this matters to you

The deals you lose here, you never see

A buyer asks an AI which company to use. It names three. If you're not one of them, nothing appears in your analytics — no visit, no bounce, no referrer. The deal was decided before you knew it existed.

Who should I hire for this?

Somebody gets named. Is it you?

Right now you almost certainly don't know. Nobody does, until they ask — which takes about four minutes and nobody does it either.

Is it worth the money for a team my size?

The model answers on your behalf

From whatever it can find. An old price, a 2023 forum complaint, a competitor's comparison page. It answers with total confidence either way.

What are the downsides?

This one costs the most

It's where models repeat things that aren't true any more — a discontinued product, a price you raised, a limitation you fixed last year.

None of that shows up in a ranking report, because none of it is a ranking. The only way to find out is to ask the engines directly and write down what they say. That's the whole job.

The gap

A perfect score and nobody can find you

The best open-source AI-search auditor is about 8,500 lines and genuinely good. Its citability engine is regex: it rewards passages that open with “X is a…”, land between 134 and 167 words, and keep pronoun density low.

That's a reasonable model of what a language model finds quotable. It is not evidence that a language model quoted you. Nowhere in that codebase — or any competing product we have taken apart — does anything ask an engine a question and check whether the client appeared in the answer.

So you get numbers that feel like measurement. A page can score 94 and be invisible. A page with broken schema can own the answer because one well-liked forum comment from 2024 keeps getting retrieved. You will not predict either from a page audit. You have to look.

Method

We publish the arithmetic, including the parts that hurt

Answer Layer — the tooling we run engagements on — is open source, MIT, on GitHub. So is the scoring methodology. You can read exactly how every number is produced before you pay us anything.

We publishWhat it admits
Share of answer is share of your prompt set Whoever picks the prompts picks the number. So the set is frozen and versioned for the whole engagement, and every report states which version produced it.
We query one named engine Not “AI search.” Source-layer findings travel between engines; position and phrasing don't. We say which engine, every time.
Presence is a fraction, never a tick These systems are non-deterministic. One run is close to worthless. Three runs gives you 3/3 versus 1/3 — and 1/3 is the cheapest thing on your list to fix.
We don't model prompt volume Nobody observes it directly. Ahrefs and Semrush model it from search demand at real scale — a legitimate method we can't match. We build prompt sets from your sales calls and support tickets and cite evidence you can go and verify. Different instrument, not a better one.

What we won't do

The refusals, in public

Off-site mentions correlate roughly 3× more strongly with AI visibility than backlinks do, which has produced a lot of quiet astroturfing. Ours is written down so you can hold us to it.

  • No fake accounts, no sockpuppets, no paid posts dressed as organic. Bans propagate, platforms flag the pattern, and a burned community is permanent.
  • No bought reviews. Asking every customer at a natural moment is legitimate and most companies simply never do it. Selecting only the happy ones is where it turns.
  • No burying a true complaint. If the models say onboarding is confusing because onboarding is confusing, that's product feedback that arrived through a language model. You get it as feedback, not as a campaign.
  • No chasing prompts you can't win. When the honest answer is that a competitor serves that buyer better, we say so and spend the hour somewhere it pays.
  • No reshuffling the prompt set to make the line go up. That's a scam with extra steps, and you'd eventually work it out.
